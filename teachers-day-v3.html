Page One

Dear Shayeesta Ma'am,

I may have left your classroom, but I don't think I’ll ever leave behind the lessons you taught me.

still true

I remember coming to your house for tuition, sitting there working through tenses and sentence structures that used to feel like a wall — and somehow, in your class, that wall always found a door.At the time, they may have felt like ordinary lessons, but looking back, I realize how meaningful they were.

There are many teachers we study with, but only a few who become a memorable part of our journey. I feel genuinely lucky to have been one of your students.You were kind in a way that didn't need to announce itself. Patient when I got things wrong. Encouraging in a way that made me actually want to get them right the next time. That kindness has stayed with me far longer than any rule of grammar has.

well remembered

I don't have a photograph from those days. But I have this — the memory of a teacher who made a difficult subject feel like an easy, welcome guest in my mind. I don't think I ever properly said thank you for it. So here it is, a little late, on a page ruled like the ones we used to write on. And I can't forget the day when I texted you on a random night long after I left the institute and yet you recognised me even without my profile picture or name. That moment really got stuck into my heart. I can't explain how special you are to me. I love you so much ma'am. As long as I exists, you'll always be loved and respected.

With more gratitude than this page can hold,

Page Two

A Few Things You Taught Me

— not all of them were in the syllabus

Rule: Kindness doesn't need a reason.

You were warm with every student, and somehow still made each one feel like your favourite.

Rule: A good teacher simplifies, never complicates.

Grammar that confused me everywhere else made perfect sense the moment you explained it.

Rule: A classroom is wherever the teacher is.

Even at your home, around a small table, every session felt exactly like a proper class.

Rule: What is taught gently is remembered longest.

Years on, I still remember how it felt to learn from you — long after I've forgotten most of the grammar.
